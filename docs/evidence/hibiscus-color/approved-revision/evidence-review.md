# Independent evidence and editorial review

## Decision

**Original framing: reject. Revised article: accept for editorial handoff, not integration/publication.** A general claim that anthocyanin retention and color need separate assessment is defensible. Making the plastic-bottle 15.3 versus 15.2 a* contrast the principal demonstration was disproportionate to the evidence.[3] The revised title removes the implied prediction that better retention fails to preserve redness; the article explains distinct measurements instead.

## Stronger evidence actually present

1. **2014, visual observation versus chemical loss:** the authors report no visually observed color change during six months at 4°C, alongside refrigerated total monomeric anthocyanin losses of 53–75% in the studied 40R fruit blends.[2] This is direct within-paper support for not inferring chemistry from appearance. It is not an equivalence test, blinded sensory panel or finding of unchanged instrumental color. Table 2 and the color discussion have mismatched numerical ranges, so the revision does not derive a quantitative color-loss comparison from those ranges.[2]
2. **2015, endpoint-specific ANOVA:** Table 5 gives temperature P < 0.001 for TMA and “ns” for a*. The a* temperature-by-time interaction is P < 0.001; packaging-by-temperature is ns, and packaging-by-time is P < 0.001.[3] A non-significant main effect is not evidence of no effect, nor can it resolve the six-month plastic-bottle pair. The revised article states the interaction instead of implying temperature-invariant redness.
3. **2015, FRAP:** the authors report losses below 30% despite marked TMA loss and offer polymeric compounds as a possible explanation.[3] This is secondary context, not independently demonstrated causation or a health claim.

## Table-type review

**Preferred in principle:** descriptive means with explicitly identified SD and sample size. **Supportable here:** means with the source's printed ± terms, identified as undefined dispersion terms, and the stated triplicate testing (n = 3).[3] No phrase in the retrieved primary XML identifies those terms as SD or SE. The original drafts' description “omitting the reported standard deviations” therefore overstates the reporting. Triplicate tests are not evidence of three independent production batches.

The revised table preserves the 2015 Table 4 40R rows for L*, a* and b*. It combines the identical before-storage entries into one labeled row; no values are averaged or recalculated. It does not reproduce inferential superscripts, because Table 4 has none. Table 1's Tukey superscripts compare formulations within a year, not the six-month Table 4 pair.[3] No chart, reconstructed error bars, ΔE threshold, digitized anthocyanin curve or statistical test has been invented.

Specific source defects retained or avoided:

- Table 4 footnote: “40R, 100% roselle”; methods, Table 1 and statistical text identify 40R as 40% extract.[3]
- Initial 40R a* is 18.3 ± 0.49 in 2012 Table 1 but 18.3 ± 0.53 in Table 4. The revision transcribes Table 4, not a silently harmonized value.[3]
- TMA units are inconsistent: 2015 methods specify cyanidin-3-glucoside equivalents per litre, while its storage discussion uses mg/100 g. Table 1's FRAP unit also differs from methods.[3] Therefore no absolute-dose interpretation, daily-intake recommendation or normalized cross-paper chemical-retention table is offered.
- The 2014 Table 2 values and prose ranges for the color axes do not consistently agree; the article uses its attributed qualitative observation and reported chemical-loss range, not a synthesized quantitative color trajectory.[2]

## Language and material boundaries

The EN/ZH revision removes the repetitive caveat/checklist pattern, including repeated notices that each proposed procurement action is not validated. It keeps one explicit safety/material boundary paragraph and local qualifications that affect interpretation. Chinese uses natural formulation language (花萼水提液、贮藏、实物参照样) rather than internal production-report phrasing. Both languages retain the same numbers, endpoint distinction and statistical limits.

The scope remains calyx-water-extract fruit beverages. It does not extend to commercial powders, an extract dosage recommendation, safe preservative use, a validated heat process, shelf-life approval, consumer health benefit or universal packaging superiority.
