## Conflicts and remaining legal gates

### L1 — Material and unresolved: whey powder versus draft qualification

Policy source [2], source-2.txt lines 56 and 67–70, says whey-powder lactose is excluded. Draft source [7], source-7.txt lines 89–96, qualifies the exclusion:

> (c) lactose in the solid constituents of whey in their preserved forms, to the extent those constituents of whey are not recombined in the drink;
> (d) lactose in the dried or concentrated form of whey, to the extent that the whey is not reconstituted in the drink.

Those are not equivalent unconditional rules. The draft cross-refers to existing regulation 7 definitions, which were not fully re-audited here. The text cannot establish that every whey powder is excluded, nor that every reconstituted whey formulation qualifies. Drafts explicitly reserve the decision. Publication remains blocked until an independent tax reviewer confirms that this limited explanatory treatment is acceptable, or a final official clarification / complete legal analysis resolves it. A generic disclaimer alone does not clear this gate.

### L2 — Resolved for wording, not treated as an alternative algorithm

Source [1] opening says “at least 4.5 grams of added sugar”; the same page’s measure section and detailed policy [2] use total sugar, with eligible lactose disregarded for milk-based drinks. Draft [7] amends the sugar-content condition accordingly. Articles use the detailed rule and never describe 4.5g as an across-the-board cap on grams of added sugar. This is an intra-HMRC textual discrepancy, not independent corroboration.

### L3 — Date discrepancy, non-load-bearing

SDIL publication [3] / detailed policy [2] say consultation closed 6 September 2026 (23:59 in [2]); Finance Bill collection [8] says 7 September. Neither consultation date is needed in public prose, so neither is used there. The discrepancy is preserved here rather than silently harmonized. Consultation closure does not establish enactment.

### L4 — Legal-status completeness not achieved

The fresh collection still describes draft legislation and says the final contents of the next Finance Bill remain subject to the Chancellor’s decision (source-8.txt lines 19–30). The latest substantive expansion document inspected was the 13 July 2026 draft. No positive verification of an enacted post-consultation expansion was obtained. Do not turn that bounded observation into “no later law exists.” Before release, independently recheck Parliament/legislation.gov.uk and the current guidance/update history, including final secondary legislation and commencement.
