# Quillaja extract for beverages: choose the grade before the delivery form

For a beverage containing oil-based flavor or color, quillaja offers a different botanical function from taste, sweetness or an active-ingredient claim: its saponins can provide emulsifying and foaming functions.[1] Start a sourcing discussion with the job the ingredient must do—disperse an oil phase, create foam, or both—not simply “quillaja powder.”

## Type 1 and Type 2 are not names for liquid and powder

JECFA’s 2005 technical assessment describes Type 1 extracts at 10–30% saponins on a dried basis and Type 2 at 65–90% on a dried basis.[1] It describes Type 2 as further purified by chromatography or ultrafiltration to reduce non-saponin soluble solids such as polyphenols and tannins.[1] These are historical material descriptions, not the guaranteed assay of a current supplier’s finished preparation.

For an oil-flavor emulsion project, include a food-use Type 2 grade in the sample discussion where reduced non-saponin solids are relevant. Do not reject a Type 1 grade solely because its saponin percentage is lower: ask for application evidence from the proposed grade. Neither the type name nor the assay establishes a universal dose, clarity result or shelf life.

| Sourcing choice | What it changes | Practical selection decision |
|---|---|---|
| Type 1 versus Type 2 | Purification and saponin concentration, rather than the physical delivery form.[1] | Specify the intended emulsifying or foaming function, then compare named grades in the actual beverage. |
| Liquid versus spray-dried powder | The assessment describes concentrate sold as liquid or spray-dried powder.[1] | Consider liquid for a liquid-dosing workflow; consider powder for a dry premix workflow. Obtain preparation instructions rather than assuming either is immediately interchangeable. |
| Extract versus standardized preparation | Food-use extracts may be diluted with lactose, maltodextrin or maltitol; preservatives may also be present.[1] | Ask for the full composition and the assay of the product as supplied, not just a percentage quoted for the underlying extract. |

## A plant source is not a complete formulation declaration

The same assessment describes possible egg-albumin processing aids and lactose carriers.[1] This does not establish that any particular current product contains egg or milk residues. It does mean that botanical origin alone is insufficient evidence for a vegan or allergen-related claim. Obtain a grade-specific ingredient declaration, processing-aid information and relevant allergen documentation before making that claim.

For liquids, request solids, saponin assay basis and preservative information. For powders, request carrier identity and level, moisture and saponin assay basis. Where a dry-basis assay is supplied, ask the supplier to state the corresponding as-supplied content before comparing doses or prices. Total solids should not be used as a substitute for measured saponins.

## What to request for an oil-flavor beverage

Send the oil or flavor identity, oil loading in the emulsion and final drink, beverage pH, carbonation, desired appearance, available homogenization process and destination market. Define an acceptable foam level as well as the appearance target. Agree which sample is being supplied: an emulsifier ingredient or a prepared flavor emulsion.

Quillaja-based beverage emulsifiers are a real commercial category: Ingredion describes its Q-NATURALE range for weighted and non-weighted beverages, including clear beverages.[2] That establishes an application route, not equivalent performance from every quillaja extract. Compare the proposed grade in the intended formula and process; do not transfer another manufacturer’s shelf-life, particle-size or certification claims.

Before purchasing, confirm the applicable food category, permitted function, use conditions and labeling requirements for the destination market. A historical technical assessment is not a current authorization for every beverage. Share the application brief for a project-specific sourcing review; grade availability, documentation and suitability require confirmation.
