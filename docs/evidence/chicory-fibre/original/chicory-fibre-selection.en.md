# Buying chicory root fibre: choose the job before the inulin grade

**Editorial draft • 22 September 2026 • Proposed intent: commercial ingredient selection for food formulators and buyers**

A clear fibre drink and a creamy reduced-fat dessert can both start with an enquiry for “chicory root fibre.” They should not start with the same assumption about the ingredient's job. BENEO divides its inulin range into standard, long-chain and oligofructose-enriched types, with different solubility, sweetness and fibre content; COSUCRA likewise sells several distinct powders and a liquid under the chicory-fibre umbrella.[1][7]

The useful purchasing question is not simply **“inulin or chicory fibre?”** It is **“which chicory-derived fructan preparation, doing what in this food, after which process?”** For a drink, the initial screening priority may be incorporation without unwanted body or visible change. For a reduced-fat dessert, the priority may instead be building the structure that a fully dissolved ingredient does not provide. These are different development briefs, not a purity ranking.

## Chicory describes the source; the grade describes the ingredient

BENEO describes its oligofructose as an inulin-type fructan obtained by partial enzymatic hydrolysis of inulin, and offers it in powder and liquid forms.[2] Its inulin portfolio separately identifies instant/high-dispersibility, highly soluble and long-chain texturizing grades.[1] Therefore, “chicory root fibre” does not distinguish a long-chain texturizer from a shorter-chain preparation intended for solubility and sweetness.[1][2]

Degree of polymerization, or DP, describes chain length. It is a useful selection variable, but not a complete performance specification: a primary study of long-chain inulin showed that preparation temperature and mixing conditions changed the properties of the resulting gels even when the material and dry-matter concentration were held constant.[8]

There is also a practical distinction between **getting powder dispersed**, **getting its molecules dissolved** and **obtaining the desired structure after cooling and storage**. BENEO's own catalogue separates an “instant” high-dispersibility grade from a “high soluble” grade.[1] Do not turn either word into an untested promise of permanent clarity, or assume that maximal dissolution is the correct objective for every food.

Two details matter more than a generic “high purity” label. First, BENEO lists separate low- and high-temperature long-chain grades.[1] Second, COSUCRA distinguishes **inulin or oligofructose on dry matter** from **dietary fibre as supplied** on the same page.[7] The buyer needs both application identity and the right quantity basis before a price comparison becomes useful.

## Why “heat it until everything dissolves” can be the wrong dessert instruction

Beccard and colleagues studied one commercial long-chain inulin, with average DP 23, in water suspensions containing 20% inulin dry matter by weight. Their preparation temperatures were 25, 40 and 60°C, with texture assessed after 24 hours at room temperature.[8] These are research conditions—not recommended food inclusion levels or industrial process settings.

In that system, higher preparation temperature increased dissolution but decreased the hardness of the subsequently developed gels. Higher mixing speed during preparation produced harder gels than the lower-speed treatment, while retaining the same temperature-related direction.[8] The authors connected the results to crystallization, the availability of seed material and the size of the resulting gel particles; they explicitly cautioned that their particle-gel model was not proven in every detail.[8]

The procurement insight is specific: **a soluble-fibre specification and a texture specification are not interchangeable acceptance criteria.** If the ingredient is being purchased to replace some of fat's structural contribution, a hot, clear premix is not by itself a successful application result. Approve the prepared food after its cooling and holding sequence, not only the incoming powder or freshly mixed liquid.

The study does not show that cooler processing is always better. It used one material in a concentrated water system, not a finished dairy dessert, plant-based spread or acidic beverage.[8] Nor does it establish that all commercial long-chain grades respond identically. Its value is to explain why sample preparation must be part of the buying brief, rather than an undocumented step between receipt and evaluation.

## Use the intended food to decide what to compare

These are proposed development choices, not results of ZL trials.

**For a clear or lightly bodied fibre drink:** start by discussing a highly soluble inulin or shorter-chain oligofructose candidate, consistent with the manufacturers' product positioning.[1][2] State the maximum acceptable body and visible change. Compare the actual drink after processing and through the intended storage conditions; do not approve it solely because the powder initially disappears in warm water. A solubility claim cannot answer what remains analytically reportable as fibre after processing.

**For a creamy reduced-fat dessert or spread:** discuss a long-chain texturizing candidate and whether it matches the available thermal route.[1][7] Keep the full-fat reference and the reduced-fat base without added fibre. Otherwise, a comparison between two fibre grades may select the firmer sample without answering whether either restores the desired eating texture. Record cooling, holding and subsequent mixing as part of the formulation—not as incidental handling.

**For a cereal-bar binder or another low-moisture system:** discuss the liquid and powder routes separately. BENEO identifies cereal binding syrups as an oligofructose application, while COSUCRA positions its liquid LCF for binding and use in low-moisture systems.[2][7] Review the water budget, mixing sequence and final product texture together. A supplier's sweetening description does not establish equal sweetness in your formula or a permitted sugar-related claim.

**For an acidic, heat-processed product:** add chemical stability to physical suitability. The accessible abstract of Vega and Zuniga-Hansen's study compared short-chain FOS in citrate buffer, orange juice and tomato juice at pH 3.5; it reports that DP and food matrix affected stability, alongside processing conditions.[9] That abstract supports matrix-specific qualification, not a universal temperature limit or a retention guarantee for long-chain chicory inulin. We did not obtain that paper's full text.

## Compare quotations on the supplied-fibre basis—then check what the process delivers

COSUCRA's public page describes FIBRULINE XL as approximately 99.5% inulin **on dry matter** and 94.5% dietary fibre **as such**. For liquid FIBRULOSE LCF, it lists approximately 90% oligofructose on dry matter and 65.5% dietary fibre as such; its fibre footnote specifies non-digestible oligo- or polymers with DP ≥3.[7] These are attributed catalogue values, not batch results, acceptance specifications or a comparative performance chart.

Do not use the dry-matter percentage as though it were the fibre fraction of each kilogram delivered. For a preliminary comparison, obtain a compatible as-supplied fibre fraction and actual quotation for each candidate:

**Quoted ingredient cost per kilogram of incoming fibre = price per kilogram of supplied ingredient ÷ as-supplied fibre mass fraction.**

This is arithmetic, not a claim that the cheapest incoming fibre gives the cheapest acceptable food. It excludes processing losses, other formulation changes, transport differences and functional performance. It also does not establish the finished product's label declaration. An equal incoming-fibre comparison and an equal final-texture comparison answer different questions; label them separately.

For US projects, FDA's dietary-fibre Q&A lists inulin and inulin-type fructans among carbohydrates it intends to propose adding to the definition, and describes enforcement discretion pending rulemaking.[3] It also explains that declared fibre must exclude quantities that do not meet the definition and that analytical methods alone cannot distinguish every qualifying from non-qualifying non-digestible carbohydrate.[3] That is more precise than calling every chicory-derived product “FDA-approved fibre.” The raw-material identity, finished-food evidence and applicable labelling rules need to agree; other markets require their own review.

## A sourcing enquiry that gives the supplier a real decision to make

For a dessert project, a useful brief would be:

> We are evaluating chicory-derived fibre for a reduced-fat spoonable dessert. Please propose a texturizing grade compatible with our heating, cooling and post-cooling mixing sequence, and explain how it differs from your highly soluble grade. Quote on the supplied-ingredient basis and identify the fibre fraction as supplied. We will compare texture against both the full-fat reference and the reduced-fat base before approving a material.

For a beverage, replace the texture-restoration objective with the required clarity/body, actual acidity and thermal process. Include destination market, intended serving, packaging, quantity and composition restrictions. These are example enquiries, not orders or validated recipes.
