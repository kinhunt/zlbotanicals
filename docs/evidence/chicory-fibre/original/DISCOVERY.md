## Three hypotheses actually explored

### 1. Inulin versus chicory fibre — selected, then reframed

**Initial hypothesis:** a material-name comparison could explain apparently competing ingredients.

**Observed evidence:** BENEO's actual catalogue separates instant ST-Gel, highly soluble HSI, and long-chain HP/HPX distinguished by processing temperature.[1] Its oligofructose page describes partial enzymatic hydrolysis and both powder/liquid forms.[2] COSUCRA independently offers Instant, XL, S30, liquid LCF and powdered oligofructose, including dry-matter versus as-supplied fibre descriptions.[7] Beccard et al.'s experimental article shows that preparation temperature and mixing alter gel hardness for one long-chain material.[8]

**What changed:** a simple “are they the same?” page would be thin. The valuable decision is whether to dissolve a fibre or develop texture, with product/process qualification attached. The catalogue's HP/HPX distinction was more useful than ranking purity. FDA's actual enforcement-discretion wording also corrects manufacturers' broad “approved fibre” wording without turning the whole article into regulatory advice.[3]

**Reader/output/commercial fit:** food R&D and procurement; full selection article with actual attributed product examples and three application branches. Project sourcing is authorized; inventory, brand rights and precise grades are not established. Priority: ready for independent factual and native-language review.

### 2. Beta-carotene beadlets versus crystalline material — defer

**Hypothesis:** higher active concentration can hide an incompatible delivery format; beadlet carrier architecture may matter more than assay for tablets versus drinks.

**Observed primary manufacturer evidence:** BASF's current beta-carotene page offers oily dispersions and powders and distinguishes synthetic nature-identical material from algae-derived material.[5] Search discovery also located BASF's microencapsulation brochure and a primary cell-culture delivery paper; search-only leads remain in `evidence/search-beta.json`, not treated as read scientific evidence.

**Why not selected:** genuine future potential, but the broad oil/dispersible comparison overlaps the existing paprika article. A distinct next article would need tablet compression versus beverage redispersion evidence and full formulation/carrier records. BASF performance statements alone cannot establish comparative stability, and cell-culture delivery cannot validate food performance. No comparative retention graph, natural-origin assumption or manufacturer-supplied unpublished chart was imported. Defer rather than publish a renamed paprika article.

### 3. Licorice DGL versus glycyrrhizin — reject as a new page

**Hypothesis:** low residual glycyrrhizin does not define the retained mixture or establish safety.

**Observed official health-source evidence:** NCCIH discusses serious glycyrrhizin-related adverse effects and uses qualified wording for oral deglycyrrhizinated licorice; its page is an official health summary, not a clinical trial or a product approval.[6]

**Why rejected:** this is already extensively covered in the site's licorice product and encyclopedia content. A new page would repeat residual assay, retained flavonoids, daily exposure and safety boundaries. No new primary separation experiment or genuinely novel commercial sample set was obtained to justify a separate article. Preserve as an existing-page maintenance topic only.
