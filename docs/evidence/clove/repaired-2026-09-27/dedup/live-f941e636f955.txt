Plant extract basics Ingredients Extraction processes Extraction equipment guide Plant extract applications Standards and regulatory references Research and industry insights Two ways to remove liquid Spray drying disperses a suitable liquid feed into droplets for drying; freeze drying removes frozen solvent through sublimation under reduced pressure. They place different demands on feed preparation and process control. Neither label alone establishes preservation of every constituent or proves that a powder meets an intended application specification. The carrier is part of the material A drying or formulation carrier may affect handling and the reported composition of a commercial powder. Ask which carrier is used, whether it is included in the declared extract ratio and how the marker assay is expressed. A free-flowing powder should not be assumed to be pure native extract, and a more expensive drying route should not be assumed superior without relevant data. Measure the result that matters Compare moisture-related specifications, marker retention, dispersibility and stability under defined packaging and storage conditions. Assess representative lots and the actual finished matrix before setting acceptance criteria. This page gives no dryer settings or promised retention percentage; those require qualified process development and product-specific verification. Sample comparison Compare like-for-like carrier and assay bases before attributing a difference solely to the drying technology. Select against an output profile, not a premium label Start with the required composition, moisture attribute, residual-solvent question, powder handling, reconstitution and packaging conditions. Ask which attributes are critical to the intended use and how they will be measured. “Freeze dried” should not function as an automatic premium grade, and “spray dried” should not function as an automatic damage claim. The comparison needs evidence for the actual extract, carrier and process. Carrier and feed checklist The wall-material review emphasizes compatibility between the material used for encapsulation and the compounds being encapsulated.[4] Ask whether the drying trials use the same extract solids and carrier basis, and whether different carriers are being compared at the same time as different dryers. Request the complete feed composition and the basis of the reported yield. A higher recovered powder mass may not answer the question of retained botanical constituents. Evidence checklist across the complete route Request feed and collected-product assays on compatible bases, relevant impurity or degradation checks, recovery accounting, handling observations and reconstitution results. Ask how wall deposits, fines and material remaining in equipment are accounted for. For freeze-dried material, include any subsequent milling or blending in the review. For spray-dried material, request the actual process record rather than treating a single inlet temperature as the complete thermal history. Scale-up and disposition checklist Before choosing a route, ask what pilot evidence supports transfer to the proposed production scale, packaging and storage conditions. Compare the complete process boundary when discussing cost or energy, including preparation, drying, recovery and any finishing steps. Retain unresolved losses or quality differences rather than declaring a universally superior dryer. No validated settings, equipment ownership, shelf-life result or ZL processing capability is implied.
