# Choosing Elderberry Extract: Why Two 7% Anthocyanin Ingredients May Be Different Materials

“7% anthocyanins” sounds like a clear specification for an elderberry extract. Yet [Iprona’s public product list](https://www.iprona.com/fileadmin/Holundersamenoel/2025/IPRONA_Lieferprogramm_Extrakte_26_web.pdf) includes both a conventional 7% grade and HighFiber 7%. Both declare at least 7% anthocyanins, while their declared elderberry fiber contents are approximately 5.2% and 43%, respectively. The same anthocyanin headline does not define the rest of the ingredient.[5]

This is not simply a choice between premium and basic grades. The intended product determines what matters: a standardized anthocyanin input, an elderberry-derived fiber component, or beverage color. Looking at actual commercial documents helps purchasing and formulation teams select the right material before comparing prices per kilogram.

## Start with composition, not the grade name

The following four entries come from the same Iprona product list. All identify the botanical source as Sambucus nigra fruit. These are supplier declarations, not results from our own batch testing.[5]

| Grade in the product list | Anthocyanins | Elderberry fiber, as described by the supplier |
|---|---:|---:|
| Conventional 7% | ≥7% | Approximately 5.2% |
| HighFiber 7% | ≥7% | Approximately 43% |
| Conventional 4% | ≥4% | Approximately 3.1% |
| HighFiber 4% | ≥4% | Approximately 54% |

Source: [5]. The list does not specify analytical methods or define the analytical basis of “elderberry fiber.” Fiber figures retain the supplier’s approximation sign; they are not finished-product nutrition values or minimum-content guarantees.

The purchasing implication is straightforward: **matching the anthocyanin grade is only part of matching the ingredient.** Conventional 7% and HighFiber 7% should enter a sample comparison as compositionally different candidates, not merely as two prices for the same powder. HighFiber 4% also has a higher listed fiber approximation than HighFiber 7%, illustrating why a larger percentage in the grade name does not mean more of every constituent.[5]

For a brand interested in elderberry-derived fiber, HighFiber offers a commercial material direction worth investigating. For a project that prioritizes clarity, reconstitution or a particular mouthfeel, the list alone cannot establish suitability: it provides no such application results. A grade-specific specification should precede formulation screening.

## Read “7%” together with its analytical method

[Artemis’s published ElderCraft 7% specification](https://artemis-nutraceuticals.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/B0120169-ElderCraft-7-Spec.pdf) illustrates why the method belongs beside the number. For the same product, B0120169, the document gives two anthocyanin specification ranges, both expressed as cyanidin-3-glucoside.[1]

| Product in the specification | Anthocyanins by pH differential, g/100 g | Anthocyanins by HPLC, g/100 g |
|---|---:|---:|
| ElderCraft 7%, B0120169 | 7.00–8.80 | 6.50–8.10 |
| ElderCraft 4%, B0120253 | 4.00–5.40 | 4.10–5.30 |

Sources: the 7% specification[1] and 4% specification[2], issued on February 6, 2023 and March 15, 2023, respectively. They illustrate specification structure; a purchase should use the supplier-confirmed current revision.

**These are method-specific acceptance ranges, not two test results for a single batch.** The 6.50 g/100 g HPLC lower limit in the 7% document does not, by itself, contradict the product name: the 7% minimum appears in the other method’s specification.[1] Nor does this document establish a universal conversion factor between the methods for other suppliers’ extracts.

Polyphenol declarations require the same care. The 7% sheet lists Folin–Ciocalteu ranges expressed as catechin and as gallic acid equivalent: 8.40–15.40 and 7.70–14.70 g/100 g, respectively.[1] A claim that one ingredient has “more polyphenols” therefore needs aligned methods, equivalents and reporting bases before the figures can be meaningfully compared.

A useful purchasing specification can be concise: anthocyanin content, analytical method, reference equivalent, unit and acceptance limit. If acceptance is on a dry basis or an as-received basis, that needs to be agreed explicitly rather than inferred from the grade name.

## A carrier-containing standardized extract can be the right ingredient

Both Artemis sheets describe a Sambucus nigra fruit extract processed by physical membrane separation and spray-dried with maltodextrin as an excipient. The 7% sheet gives an extract ratio of 32:1; the 4% sheet gives 16–18:1.[1][2] These are standardized extracts with a declared excipient—not mutually exclusive categories.

The documents describe the powders as hygroscopic, with a characteristic elderberry taste and astringency, and soluble in water and water/alcohol mixtures.[1][2] Those details help describe what a formulator receives more directly than an extract ratio alone. The whole powder enters the recipe, not just the anthocyanins reported by the laboratory.

A useful sample comparison has two views. First, compare equal powder additions to see the direct effect on the existing recipe. Then, once methods and content bases are aligned, compare the powder quantities needed for the same intended anthocyanin input and account for changes to the rest of the formulation. This is a proposed selection approach, not evidence that the materials are equivalent.

“Carrier-free” should not automatically outrank a declared maltodextrin-containing grade. The finished product’s ingredient requirements, process and sensory target come first. For the HighFiber grades, the documents obtained here do not establish the complete excipient composition; the conventional grades’ water-solubility descriptions must not be transferred to them.

## Three retail listings show what product names leave unresolved

Retail products show brands how an ingredient story reaches the customer, but a listing title is not a complete specification. Three Amazon product details illustrate different limits to what those descriptions establish.

### Terrasoul: freeze-drying and no additives, rather than a standardized assay

[Terrasoul Superfoods](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B086BXMF5X) names its product a freeze-dried elderberry powder. Its product bullet says it is made from “100% elderberries with no additives.”[10] This emphasizes fruit source and processing; that statement does not provide an anthocyanin standardization percentage.[10]

For a brand interested in a fruit-led product, this offers a different direction from a 7% standardized extract. Purchasing should preserve the distinction: freeze-drying describes processing, not an assay grade. It does not establish that the powder can replace a standardized ingredient at equal weight for a fixed anthocyanin input.

### Bulk Essentials: 175 mg per serving is not 175 mg of anthocyanins

The [Bulk Essentials](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FF6GT2NY) title reads “Elderberry Extract Powder w/Maltodextrin, 250g, 175mg Per Serving.” It explicitly names an extract, maltodextrin and a per-serving quantity, but does not identify the 175 mg as anthocyanins.[11]

That is a different kind of information from a raw-material specification of 7.00–8.80 g/100 g by the pH-differential method. A brand may organize finished-product communication around a serving; purchasing still needs to establish what that amount represents and how the relevant constituent is measured. A per-serving quantity from a title does not belong in an anthocyanin assay field.

### Food to Live: one page gives conflicting material descriptions

The [Food to Live](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B097YTRXG5) title calls the product “Black Elderberry Juice Powder” and says “Contains Maltodextrin.” Yet a product bullet on the same ASIN says “Unlike JUICE Elderberry Powder” and describes the powder as made from “entire berries.”[9] The title places it in a juice-powder category while the detail distinguishes it from juice powder; combining those statements does not establish a single material identity.

The discrepancy was found in the product-detail capture and reproduced in the title and product-bullet regions of the same ASIN’s public HTML. It establishes inconsistent listing text—not the physical contents of the package or adulteration. For purchasing, the practical response is to obtain the current ingredient declaration, product specification and batch documentation before placing it in either a whole-fruit-powder or juice-powder price comparison.

Across these examples, processing, excipients, serving amounts and constituent standardization appear at different levels of the commercial description. Separating them makes it easier to decide which materials belong in the same sample comparison.

## Choose candidates for the finished-product task

### A standardized elderberry supplement: align identity and assay basis

For capsule, tablet or powder projects, standardized black elderberry fruit extracts can form the first candidate group, with the appropriate grade selected around the complete formulation. Here, the anthocyanin percentage defines a raw-material input and quality requirement, not an efficacy dose.

Identity deserves its own assessment. Alongside content specifications, both Artemis sheets list an “HPLC anthocyanin profile unique to elderberry” as an identity item.[1][2] A 2023 elderberry review also discusses chromatographic and hyphenated techniques for qualitative and quantitative analysis and chemical profiling.[8] Content addresses how much is present; identity evidence addresses what the material is. One aggregate percentage should not stand in for both.

### An elderberry-fiber concept: make fiber a separate development objective

HighFiber 4% and 7% represent a different commercial direction: their listed material characteristics include substantially higher approximate elderberry fiber contents alongside standardized constituents.[5] That supports a brand-concept and sample discussion. Whether a finished product can carry a “high fiber” claim still depends on the target market’s rules, the final recipe and appropriate analytical results.

At this stage, formulation work should establish whether the finished product benefits from that material at the intended addition level: dispersion, mouthfeel and appearance are practical acceptance criteria. A larger fiber declaration is not evidence of beverage stability, clarity or a particular consumer experience.

### Beverage coloring: a color ingredient is a different purchasing task

In its food and beverage business, Iprona also identifies [Rubini® as a coloring solution](https://www.iprona.com/en/our-procuts-and-services) made from black elderberry and states that its natural colorants use industry-standard unit quantification.[7] Elderberry therefore appears in commercial uses beyond supplement ingredients purchased on an anthocyanin percentage.

When the main task is beverage coloring, start with the target shade, addition rate in the actual recipe, flavor contribution and shelf-life performance. Then establish identity, composition and local labeling requirements. A higher anthocyanin percentage on a supplement extract does not automatically make it the better-value color for that drink. This is a distinction between purchasing tasks, not a claim that Rubini and ElderCraft are interchangeable.

## Compare costs in the recipe, not just per kilogram

Price becomes comparable after the material direction is selected. For a standardized-extract project, align identity, assay method, content and excipients before estimating the ingredient cost needed to meet the formulation input target. For a color project, compare use levels that meet the same finished-product color requirement. A fiber project also needs the intended fiber contribution and any changes to other ingredients considered.

Public product lists cannot supply those answers automatically. Without current quotations, batch assays or finished-product trials, there is no evidence-based “best-value” ranking here. The useful principle is to avoid buying a cheaper kilogram that performs a different job in the recipe.

## About the evidence

Supplier documents and three Amazon product details were inspected on September 21, 2026. The listings illustrate differences in material descriptions, not market distribution. The initial eight-result search included bilberry and syrup; only the three elderberry powder details subsequently inspected are discussed here. Listing statements are not independent verification of composition, certification or nutritional effects. Artemis and Iprona documents concern the same branded ingredient system. Complete HighFiber TDSs, batch COAs and finished-product trials were not obtained.
