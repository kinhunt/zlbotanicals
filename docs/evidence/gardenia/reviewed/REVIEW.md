## Decisive findings

1. **Allowed uses versus requested expansion: correct, with a chronology improvement.** The six listed categories are sport drinks, flavored/enhanced noncarbonated water, fruit drinks and ades, ready-to-drink teas, hard candy and soft candy, under GMP and the standards-of-identity restriction. The approval was published July 15, 2025, became effective August 29, and was confirmed October 1 after no objections/hearing requests. The October confirmation is not a second authorization date.[1][7]

2. **CAP 6C0343 is not an approval.** FDA's independently retrieved record says “under review,” with filing/status date August 4, 2026; the notice was published August 19. The notice enumerates 20 numbered proposed-use entries, including carbonated drinks and powdered beverages. Some numbered entries group multiple foods, so “20 additional uses” is acceptable shorthand for those entries, not 20 individually defined commodity approvals. In Chinese, replace “申请于8月4日提交” with “FDA于8月4日将该申请立案”: FDA's filing date does not establish when the petitioner initially submitted its package.[2][3]

3. **Arsenic numbers and denominator: correct.** Existing text says ≤2 mg/kg; the expansion petition proposes ≤1 mg/kg. These are color-additive material specifications, not finished-beverage doses or intake limits. The draft does not invent a recommended use rate.[1][2]

4. **Methanol discrepancy is now resolved, not a continuing unexplained contradiction.** The original July order printed 6 mg/kg; the official August 6 correction explicitly replaces it with 300 mg/kg and shares the August 29 effective date. This is a corrected misstatement, not a 2026 relaxation inferred from an eCFR discrepancy. The revised article includes a short useful specification example; REPORT.zh.md's statement that the correction was not fully retrieved is superseded by this review.[4][6]

5. **Soy assay, labeling exemption and batch certification are separate.** The 2025 order reports the petitioner's nondetect immunoassay and separately states the labeling requirement absent an exemption. It records the September 29, 2022 allergen petition as then under review. No analytical detection threshold, absolute soy absence or applicability to every supplier lot can be derived from that narrative. The revised article explicitly dates both the labeling discussion and pending-petition statement to July 15, 2025. It does not say the allergen petition remains pending in September 2026, nor that it was granted. Regulatory batch-certification exemption is neither a soy exemption nor supplier/facility certification nor proof that a batch meets specifications.[1][4][5]

6. **Consolidation uncertainty remains bounded.** Fresh eCFR extraction shows the August 6 amendment, the original six uses, arsenic 2 and methanol 300 mg/kg, but no explicit consolidation-through date. A URL containing `/current/` and today's retrieval do not prove legal completeness through today. The FDA status/inventory records corroborate the article's specific claims; the FDA database's own status date remains August 4 rather than September 27.[1][3][5]
The correction and confirmation supply the official chronology.[6][7]
Revised prose states the extraction limit once in a source-method paragraph rather than repeatedly interrupting the analysis.

7. **No unsupported categorical ban.** The originals correctly stop short of declaring every carbonated product unlawful: some complex formulations need individual category analysis. Retain that distinction. The conclusion is that the pending expansion does not itself authorize a use and a noncarbonated-water listing cannot simply be generalized.[1][2]
