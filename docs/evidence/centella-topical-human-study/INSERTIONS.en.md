## effects | A human moisturizing study of a cream and hydrogel

In a four-week study, 25 healthy volunteers applied an oil-in-water (O/W) cream or hydrogel containing Centella extract to their forearms twice daily. The researchers compared the formulations with their extract-free bases and untreated areas, measuring stratum corneum hydration and transepidermal water loss (TEWL). Of the 2.5% and 5% extract levels described in the study text, the authors reported the best moisturizing results with the 5% formulations: hydration increased and TEWL decreased.[S15]

The material description matters here. The purchased Herbalia® Centella dried extract was described as containing 10% active constituents, naming madecassic acid, asiatic acid and asiaticoside; madecassoside content was not separately reported. Although the study text specifies 2.5% and 5%, both recipe tables list 3%. The relationship between a separate ethanol-extraction step and the purchased dry extract is also unclear. The study therefore offers moisturizing observations for particular topical formulations, rather than a universal Centella use level or a directly reproducible finished-product recipe.[S15]

## insights | Match the material to the measured outcome

For formulation development, “Centella extract” needs to be narrowed down to its composition and vehicle. The percentages below describe different things; comparing their size alone does not establish which ingredient is stronger.

| Material in the source | What the percentage describes | What it contributes to formulation assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial extract described in the moisturizing study | The materials section lists three active constituents totaling 10%; the study text describes 2.5% and 5% formulation loadings, inconsistent with the 3% recipe tables | Provides context for the study's hydration and TEWL observations. Multiplying the percentages does not establish a verified finished-formula active content.[S15] |
| Standardized extract in US8486900B2 | Madecassoside, terminoloside and asiaticoside together account for at least 75 wt% of the final extract | The assay needs to distinguish terminoloside from madecassoside; this threshold is not a cream loading.[S08] |
| Extract in claim 1 of US10980851B2 | About 20–60% asiaticoside and about 40–80% combined triterpenic acids, by extract weight | Check glycosides and aglycones separately. The composition also includes oat extract, ceramide and other ingredients; Centella concentration alone does not establish formula equivalence.[S09] |

For a proposed ingredient, first check plant part, extraction solvent, dry solids and carrier, then compare individual markers on the same analytical basis. Once the materials are sufficiently comparable, check whether the study used a cream, gel or oral preparation, and whether it measured hydration, TEWL or another outcome. This connects the research to the actual formulation rather than merely matching a plant name.

## patents | Ratio notes for the existing patent summaries

**US8486900B2:** In claim 1, “at least 75 wt%” is relative to the total weight of the final extract. The “more than 95 wt%” requirement for the second extract used in standardization refers to madecassoside and terminoloside combined, relative to that second extract's weight. Claim 5 separately gives an asiaticoside:(madecassoside + terminoloside) mass ratio of 5:95 to 25:75; this is not a fixed asiaticoside percentage of the whole extract.[S08]

**US10980851B2:** Claim 1 distinguishes about 0.0001–1.5% extract in the overall composition from about 20–60% asiaticoside and about 40–80% combined acids within the extract. It explicitly specifies an oil-in-water (O/W) carrier. These are ranges within a claimed technical composition, not universal effective use levels. The specification defaults to the active portion of components, so the percentages cannot simply be applied to the addition rate of any commercial diluted ingredient.[S09]
