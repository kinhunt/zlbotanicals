## Scientific acceptance, claim by claim

- **Materials and counts:** [1] Samples lists 23 stigma samples plus seven powders, eight botanical groups described as species, and separate pomegranate petals/seeds. Keep the paper's eight-species shorthand; Capsicum is actually reported as “spp.”, so do not upgrade this to eight taxonomically resolved species. There are nine challenged material groups, not nine botanical species. [1]
- **Preparation:** [1] Sample preparation gives ethanol/water 7:3 v/v, sonication/centrifugation, pooled stigma and separately pooled adulterant materials, 90/10, 95/5, 97/3, 99/1 w/w mixtures, triplicate mixture preparation. Ordinary samples were prepared in duplicate. Neither draft mislabels triplicates as independent production lots. The revised text explicitly distinguishes analytical extraction from commercial manufacture of an extract ingredient. [1]
- **1% detection:** [1] Method development reports all investigated plants detectable at 1% w/w through at least three marker ions with S:N ≥10:1. This is a lowest-tested controlled addition result, not a demonstrated universal analytical LOD, sensitivity rate, certified 99% purity, or validated release limit. The revision explicitly attributes it to the authors; the result has not been independently reproduced here. [1]
- **>80% classification:** [1] Evaluation of unknown samples specifies **more than** 80% of one adulterant's markers, based on the Pareto principle. Preserve >, not ≥. The denominator is that adulterant's marker set, not 82 total library markers and not adulterant mass. Retain this useful point: the paper's detection and classification statements answer different questions. Neither the original nor revision proves that the rule failed at 1%; the revision makes the absence of that inference clearer. The authors separately require all saffron markers with comparable relative abundance for their authenticity assessment; the article does not present the adulterant rule as a complete executable SOP. [1]
- **82 markers and seven negatives:** 82 includes saffron/adulterant markers; it is not 82 species or necessarily 82 fully confirmed structures. The market section says all saffron markers were detected and no monitored adulterant markers were detected in seven Czech-market powders. These were the same seven powders included in the study, not a further external validation set. The draft's narrower negative wording is preferable to the paper's categorical “not adulterated.” No fraud prevalence, representative-market estimate, or universal purity inference is supported. [1]
- **UV grade versus authenticity:** [2] Materials/Table 2 establishes 21 samples: nine powders/twelve filaments, commercial/private origins. Results §3.2 gives 13 unclassified, nine of those solely due to odorous power. §3.3 reports saffron-like FTIR features while §3.4 reports floral contamination in some powders. These support complementary observations, not a proof of all-round purity or an allegation of fraud. “Odorous power” is an optical measurement, not a sensory score; EN now says optical explicitly, as ZH already did. Only three selected samples received ICP-OES (§3.6); “selected subset” is correct. [2]
- **Source inconsistencies:** [2] introduction gives 270 nm versus methods/equations 257 nm; discussion overgeneralizes filament classification despite the specific §3.2 results. These are accurately identified by the discovery audit. Neither should become a reconstructed ISO SOP, silently corrected archive, universal claim that grading is useless, or a chart. Salt/sand intentional-adulteration speculation and unverified heavy-metal compliance conclusions remain excluded. [2]
- **Gardenia:** appears in [1] background/referenced work, not its experimental eight-group botanical panel. Citation alone cannot show a supplier's gardenia coverage. Do not use the paper's literature-review method table to create an unaudited comparative detection ranking. [1]
- **Powder/extract transfer and commercial scope:** the evidence concerns spice materials and laboratory extracts made from them, not general validation for manufactured concentrates, carriers, or finished foods. The procurement request for actual-matrix applicability is an editorial recommendation, not a demonstrated extract defect or a guarantee of available testing service. Formulation colour/aroma/taste/dispersion/stability checks are clearly proposed project criteria, with no invented result, threshold, stock, MOQ, certification or health claim.
