# Decision: retain hops, select aroma-led material choice for alcohol-free beer

Status: substantive research/draft package, NOT publish-ready. Independent original-source and EN/ZH editorial gate remains mandatory. No repo/backlog changes, BrowserMan activity or publication.

## Three competing interpretations

1. **Bitterness adjustment:** relevant because the current isomerized-extract sheet describes a specific aqueous bitter-acid material and a concentrated premix precipitation warning. Useful supporting contrast, but a standalone generic bitterness worksheet would repeat the site's sensory/process template. Not selected as the principal article.[7]
2. **Aroma selection in alcohol-free beer:** SELECTED FREE is explicitly positioned for this application, specifies a propylene-glycol carrier and undetectable bittering substances, and characterizes its polar aroma fraction. A real supplier/university pilot study shows why all hop constituents cannot be treated as equally alcohol-dependent. SELECTED as the lead research opportunity.[5][3]
3. **Lightstruck protection:** Tetra's technical sheet imposes whole-process exclusions for alpha/iso-alpha acids. Strong bounded purchasing distinction, but no retrieved finished-beverage light challenge establishes shelf life. Retain as a contrast section; defer a separate quantitative packaging/light-stability article.[6]

One EN/ZH piece, not three thin pages. Narrow the initial nonalcoholic-drink concept to beer: evidence does not validate 0.0% hop water. No need to switch botanicals because hops has direct application evidence.

## Quantitative audit and contradictions

Actual PDF printed page 4 rendered to aroma-table-page.png and visually checked. Four rows and all original column identities are in table-conditions.json, including the original base n.d. entries. Printed table label is Table 3, while prose calls it Table 4. Printed unit ends at μG/; nearby prose provides µg/l context, not permission for silent repair. Drafts disclose both defects.

Geraniol's highest-ABV value is lower, and the author's sampling-error suggestion is not verified. Myrcene's exceptionally high values are attributed to closed, low-yeast conditions, not a transferable beverage benchmark. Source beta-acid prose says below 6.4% while its table includes 6.4%; broad humulinone conclusions obscure low-group differences; limonene identity differs between table and later prose. These latter analytes are deliberately not developed into draft claims. No significant-difference claims are inferred from table coloring or a mean ratio.

Supplier lightstruck protection and aroma aging statements describe different endpoints. The former is conditional; the latter has no exposed study duration/light/packaging data. Preserve both without treating them as a contradiction or as measured shelf life.
