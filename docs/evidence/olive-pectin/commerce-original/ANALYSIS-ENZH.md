## Publication-ready English passage

### What three olive-leaf listings tell a formulator—and what they do not

A small US Amazon sample collected on 23 September 2026 shows why “olive leaf extract” is not a finished-product specification. We selected three listings to examine different ways of presenting the ingredient: NOW’s 120-count capsules, Wild & Organic’s 4 fl oz liquid drops, and Island Nutrition’s 90-count capsules combining leaf and fruit extracts. These are examples of positioning, not a representative survey. In particular, “liquid” and “drops” describe the same Wild & Organic product, while the blended product is itself a capsule. Dosage form and ingredient composition are separate decisions.

[NOW’s listing](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0019LPMDY) makes a defined capsule amount and minimum standardization easy to find. Its gallery label declares 500 mg olive leaf extract per vegetarian capsule, standardized to a minimum of 6% oleuropein. The formulation also lists a cellulose-derived capsule shell, vegetable-source stearic acid and silicon dioxide. For a material buyer, the useful distinction is between the declared extract quantity, the marker specification and the finished capsule’s other ingredients. A milligram figure on the front is not a complete raw-material brief, and a printed minimum is not a certificate of analysis for the bottle or an assurance of clinical performance.

[Wild & Organic’s liquid](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0C15XRRDJ) puts the experience of taking the supplement closer to the center of its pitch. The page emphasizes an alcohol-free vegetable-glycerin base, natural sweetness without added sugar, and mixing the drops into a drink. The displayed label lists 500 mg organic olive leaf extract per 0.7 ml serving, with certified organic vegetable glycerin and purified water as other ingredients. It does not show an extract ratio or an oleuropein standardization. That omission limits comparison: the matching “500 mg” on these two products does not establish matching marker content or equivalent effects. The dropper presentation also adds practical development questions about serving-volume repeatability, taste, dilution and microbiological control. Those are questions to test in a formulation project, not performance results supplied by this sample. One gallery graphic advertises “98% of absorption in 2–5 minutes” without a visible supporting source; we therefore record it as marketing, not evidence that drops absorb better than capsules.

[Island Nutrition’s product](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B010YH0BS4) illustrates a different distinction hidden inside the familiar capsule format. Its rear-bottle label and separate Supplement Facts artwork both list 500 mg olive leaf extract and 50 mg olive fruit extract per capsule. The leaf portion is described as standardized to at least 25% oleuropein and 40% total olive polyphenols; the fruit portion carries separate specifications of at least 16% pure hydroxytyrosol and 20% total hydroxytyrosol complex. The front-of-pack percentages should therefore not be applied indiscriminately to the whole capsule. Nor should oleuropein and total-polyphenol figures be added as though they were independent ingredient masses. This is a leaf-plus-fruit formulation, not simply a larger number attached to the same leaf extract. The rear label also distinguishes “Grown and Extracted in Spain” from “Encapsulated and Bottled in USA”—two supply-chain steps that a procurement specification should keep separate.

The practical lesson is to compare like with like before discussing a finished-product concept. Record plant part, extract quantity and its basis, marker identity and analytical method, carrier or other ingredients, and the intended delivery format. For a liquid concept, establish what the declared extract quantity represents and test the proposed carrier system; for a multi-extract capsule, specify each fraction separately instead of relying on a combined front-label headline. These retail observations help frame those questions. They do not demonstrate that one product is safer, more effective or more bioavailable, and search placement, customer-review counts and label serving sizes cannot establish sales share or clinical equivalence.

## 可融入正文的中文段落

### 三款橄榄叶商品，分别在强调什么？

2026年9月23日，我们通过实际浏览美国 Amazon，选取了三款橄榄叶相关商品：NOW 的120粒胶囊、Wild & Organic 的4液体盎司滴剂，以及 Island Nutrition 的90粒叶提取物与果实提取物复配胶囊。选择它们是为了观察不同的产品表达，而不是统计市场格局。首先需要厘清分类：这里的“液体”和“滴剂”是同一款产品的两种描述；“复配”说的是配方组成，不是独立剂型，复配产品同样可以装进胶囊。剂型与原料构成，不能混成一条比较轴。

[NOW 这款商品](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0019LPMDY)把每粒用量和最低标准化指标放在了显眼位置。初始图库中的瓶身标签写明，每份为1粒植物胶囊，含500 mg橄榄叶提取物，橄榄苦苷标准化含量不低于6%；其他配料包括羟丙甲纤维素胶囊、植物来源硬脂酸和二氧化硅。对原料采购而言，这种表达有助于拆开三个问题：用了多少提取物、提取物按什么指标控制，以及制成胶囊还需要哪些辅料。但正面的毫克数不是完整的原料规格，标签上的最低含量也不能代替该批次的检测报告，更不能直接证明成品功效。

[Wild & Organic 滴剂](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0C15XRRDJ)更突出服用方式和口感。商品页强调植物甘油基底、不含酒精、不额外添加糖，以及可以加入饮品的使用场景。实际查看的标签标示每份0.7 ml，含500 mg有机橄榄叶提取物，其他配料为经有机认证的植物甘油和纯化水；所见标签没有给出提取比例或橄榄苦苷标准化指标。因此，它与另一款产品同样出现“500 mg”，并不意味着标志性成分含量相同，更不能据此判定效果等同。滴管形式还带来一组应在开发中验证的问题：每次取液量是否稳定、稀释后口感如何、载体是否适配，以及如何控制微生物风险。这些是本次观察引出的研发问题，不是已经得到验证的性能结论。图库中另有“98% of absorption in 2–5 minutes”的宣传语，但图中没有展示支持来源；这里仅将其记录为营销主张，不用它证明滴剂比胶囊吸收更好。

[Island Nutrition 这款胶囊](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B010YH0BS4)则说明，即使剂型相同，内部的原料组合也可能不同。其瓶背照片与独立的成分表图片均列出：每粒含500 mg橄榄叶提取物和50 mg橄榄果实提取物。叶提取物分别标示橄榄苦苷不低于25%、总橄榄多酚不低于40%；果实提取物另列纯羟基酪醇不低于16%、总羟基酪醇复合物不低于20%。这些比例各有对应的原料，不能把正面几个百分数都套到整粒胶囊上，也不能把橄榄苦苷与总多酚当成彼此独立的质量重复相加。这是叶与果实两部分组成的配方，不只是同一种橄榄叶提取物换了一个更大的宣传数字。瓶背还将“西班牙种植并提取”与“美国装胶囊并灌装”分开表述；采购文件同样应区分原料来源、提取地点与成品加工地点。

对配方与采购团队而言，这组小样本最有价值的地方，不在于选出哪款“更强”，而在于提醒我们把比较条件写清楚：植物部位、提取物用量及其表示依据、标志性成分与检测方法、载体或辅料，以及最终的给药或食用形式。准备做液体产品时，应先问清标签提取物量的具体含义，再验证载体体系；准备做多提取物胶囊时，则应分别约定各部分原料，而不是用正面的一句含量宣传替代配方规格。本次观察可以帮助形成这些问题，却不能证明哪款更安全、更有效或生物利用度更高。搜索位置、评论数和标签每份用量，也都不能用来推算销量份额或临床等效性。
