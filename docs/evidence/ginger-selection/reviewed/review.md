# Independent ginger factual and bilingual review

## Verdict: PASS for corrected text; publication not performed

The corrected `draft.en.md` and `draft.zh.md` pass this bounded factual/editorial review against the four archived raw XML papers. This verdict applies only to the SHA-256 identities in `manifest.json`. The original drafts were generally cautious and substantially supported; the finite revisions below improve numerical specificity, compound identity and the distinction between sensory interpretation and instrumental measurement. No original, website or publication files were edited.

This is an archive-based source review, not a fresh publisher/correction-status search. Four XML bodies, their relevant methods, exact table cells, table footnotes and figure captions were independently parsed. Figure images and external supplements were not retrieved or visually audited. No numerical values were read from plot images or supplementary tables into the corrected drafts. The 150°C time experiment and oil comparison are supported by the main-text results plus figure captions, not independent digitization of those figures.

## Claim-by-claim findings

### [4] Slice drying, shogaols and recoverable oil — PASS within study scope

- Section 3.2 specifies 500 g sliced rhizome, an electric convection oven at 120/150/180°C and final moisture 10 ± 0.2%. Drying durations therefore differ by treatment; this is not a temperature-only, equal-time experiment. The draft does not convert it into a beverage process recommendation.
- Table 1 directly supports lower gingerols and higher shogaols for each hot-air group versus open sun drying. All three shogaol rows peak at 150°C among the listed hot-air conditions and decrease at 180°C. For example, the 6-shogaol row is 1.54 ± 0.06 (OSD), 2.05 ± 0.13 (STD), 3.02 ± 0.78 (120°C), 3.93 ± 0.53 (150°C), 2.58 ± 0.11 (180°C). **The XML table, caption and footnote do not state a concentration unit.** These bare values must not become mg/g or a procurement specification. The corrected public draft retains only the supported direction.
- Section 2.2 states the increase from one to six hours and decline at seven hours; Figure 2 caption identifies 150°C and triplicate measurements. No universal optimum or measured degradation mechanism is claimed.
- Section 2.3 and Figure 3 caption support OSD/STD above HAD at 150°C, with no significant OSD–STD difference. Section 3.5 uses 100 g dried ginger, 1 L water, five-hour Clevenger hydrodistillation. This is recoverable oil, not human aroma intensity or the percentage of aroma retained in a drink. No oil-yield percentage is invented.

### [2] Australian growing-year samples — PASS; denominator made explicit

- Section 2.1 describes three Queensland growing-year samples, not controlled ageing of a common starting lot. Quality labels are the authors’ anecdotal/organoleptic classifications, not independently validated grades.
- Section 2.2 specifies triplicate 90% methanol extraction and mg/kg as-is. Table 1 verifies 6-gingerol 2,215 ± 101 / 3,491 ± 26 / 5,383 ± 270 and 6-shogaol 836 ± 50 / 1,063 ± 19 / 709 ± 15 for 2017/2018/2019. Added a compact bilingual table with mean ± SD, n = 3 and **dried powder as-is**, not fresh ginger or moisture-corrected dry matter. Analytical replicates are not three independent harvest lots per year.
- Sections 2.2 and 2.5 establish DCM extraction and no internal standard; the authors explicitly call peak-area analysis semi-quantitative. Table 2 percentages are fractions of total chromatographic peak area, not mass fractions or odor contributions.
- Table 2 verifies zingiberene, geraniol and 6-shogaol as selected examples. Chinese retains English identifiers for 姜烯 and 香叶醇; 姜烯 (zingiberene) is not 姜烯酚 (shogaol). No citrus/freshness character is assigned to a molecule from its presence alone. The summed-area ranking is supported by section 3.2, but does not rank sensory quality.

### [1] Extraction, specific constituents and taste descriptions — PASS with limited numerical use

- Methods 2.2.1–2.2.3 distinguish fresh squeezed juice, 5 g dried powder infused in 95 mL water at 80°C for 30 minutes, and hydrothermal/pressure routes with and without enzymes. The pressure stage is 100 MPa, 50°C, two hours; subsequent 93°C for one hour and five-minute boiling steps remain in the referenced procedure. The draft now spells these out rather than implying nonthermal processing.
- Table 1 confirms GT 16.26%, HWE 55.40%, HPE 67.60%. Section 2.3 says dry-weight basis relative to starting raw ginger/HDP, but its wording of the division is awkward and it does not fully explain corrections for enzyme solids. Retained these only as **reported extraction yields** for powder routes, not marker purity, quantified ginger-derived solids recovery, or a fresh-juice efficiency comparison.
- Table 2 verifies all six individual 6-/8-/10-gingerol/shogaol values are higher in HWE/HPE than GT. Its 6G values (GT 1.27, HWE 7.03, HPE 6.30 mg/g) reproduce approximately 5.5× and 5.0× within the table. The exact identity of the gram denominator is not adequately clarified in the inspected method/table, so those mg/g values and multiples are not added to the public drafts. The Australian as-is HPLC values are not compared numerically against them.
- Original sentence: “Second, the paper's flavour descriptions came from instrumental analysis.” This is true but too broad. Corrected to distinguish fruity/fresh descriptors derived from electronic-nose analysis from bitterness interpretations based on measured free amino acids and assigned taste properties. Table 3 is chemistry, not a tasting panel. The methods report no human panel.
- The E-nose section reports its largest summed area for HWE, while the SBSE-TD/GC-MS/MS section reports HPE. Figure 3D caption marks the HWE–HPE total-area comparison nonsignificant. Neither platform establishes that a treatment tastes better or smells more like fresh ginger. No winner is inferred in the corrected article.
- Specific citral/neral, citronellal, zingiberenol or zingerone aroma rankings in the article’s discussion depend partly on supplements and authors’ interpretation. The corrected drafts do not inherit those rankings, equate VIP discrimination with odor potency, or translate peak area into perceived aroma intensity.

### [3] Spray drying and carriers — PASS as bounded formulation example

- Table 1 confirms that carrier amount, added water and inlet/outlet temperature vary together. No isolated carrier effect can be inferred. “No carrier” also does not mean no added water: SD_pure contains 137 g juice and 100 g added water in Table 1.
- Section 3.1 supports the fine/free-flowing starch formulation, poor powder recovery and pump blockage for the tested pea formulation, and insufficient pure-juice powder for moisture measurement. These remain attributed observations. No universal pea-protein failure, commercial impossibility, measured solubility superiority or shelf-life guarantee is claimed.
- Methods 2.3, 2.5 and 2.6 describe small-sample solvent extraction, flow-infusion electrospray MS without chromatographic separation, normalized/log2-transformed data and tentative Level 2 identifications. Figure 3 names 10-shogaol and zingerone among the relative signals. The corrected draft identifies the method and compounds without treating these as absolute assays, confirmed structural identities, odor contribution or retention mass balance.
- The inherited draft correctly flagged a ratio conflict but did not show it. Calculation from Table 1 and 4.92% juice solids: 137 × 0.0492 = 6.7404 g ginger solids; 39.36 / 6.7404 = **5.8394**, approximately **5.84 g inulin per gram ginger solids**. Section 3.1 instead says 2:1, and Table 2 labels the powder “Inulin 3:1” without specifying the basis. Both corrected languages now give the actual conflict.
- Additional internal check: treating listed carriers as dry for nominal recipe arithmetic gives ginger-solid fractions of 4.69% (starch), 15.41% (pea) and 14.62% (inulin). The latter two do not reproduce the narrative ~25–33%. These are not measured recovered-powder compositions; carrier moisture and selective process loss are unresolved. Therefore no such powder percentage is recommended or inserted into the public draft.
