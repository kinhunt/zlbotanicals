# Selection and evidence scope

Read morning continuation before retrieval. Existing archive's unavailable ginger roasting/dissertation methods remain unavailable; this draft does not claim to recover them. A different, open-primary-evidence question was selected: buying processed ginger when aroma, pungent markers and powder handling point to different choices.

## Competing explanations considered before drafting

1. Thermal conversion: gingerols convert into shogaols, but formation can compete with further loss. Strong direct drying evidence in PMC6099745; do not turn extreme slice-drying conditions into a beverage or roasting recipe.
2. Aroma loss versus altered extraction: recoverable essential oil can decrease while extracted marker concentrations increase. PMC6099745 tests oil yield; PMC8871348 compares aqueous extraction routes. Neither supplies human aroma intensity scores.
3. Starting material/history: between-lot quality or harvest differences can look like a heating effect. PMC8427268 has three different growing-year samples, not a controlled aging experiment.
4. Carrier dilution, drying conditions and analytical response: PMC12195494 varies carrier amount, added water and temperature together. Its mass-spectral intensities are not a recovery balance. Useful as a caution about comparing powders, not proof a carrier causes better pungency or aroma.

Selected because these explanations yield concrete ingredient-selection decisions, rather than another generic assay worksheet. No market-size/search-volume claim is made. No new GSC or commerce collection was attempted under this task's restrictions.

## Primary sources retrieved

[1] PMC8871348 (2022): Korean ginger, fresh squeezed juice and hot-air-dried powder-derived aqueous extracts; hydrothermal/high-pressure, with/without enzymes. UPLC, E-nose, SBSE-TD/GC-MS/MS. Instrumental descriptions are not a tasting panel. Pressure route also includes later heat steps. Do not repeat authors' 'removed bitter taste' as sensory proof. Article introduction contains chemical naming/classification problems; not used as authority for nomenclature.

[2] PMC8427268 (2021): three Queensland processing-grade ginger samples from growing years 2017, 2018, 2019; HPLC concentrations on as-is basis; solvent-extract GC-MS semi-quantification without internal standard. Quality labels from anecdotal information and organoleptic testing; no detailed calibrated sensory protocol. No controlled drying or storage comparison, no current market survey.

[3] PMC12195494 (2025): Welsh-grown ginger juice, five spray-drying formulations. HR-FIE-MS relative intensities, tentative Level 2 identification; formulations vary carrier mass, dilution and thermal conditions. Article Table 1 (137 g juice, 4.92% solids, 39.36 g inulin) is inconsistent with the Results 2:1 inulin-to-ginger-solids description and ~25–33% ginger claim. At stated solids, 137×0.0492=6.7404 g ginger solids; these figures do not support those ratios. Figure captions also say inulin 3:1. Do not silently fix or reproduce carrier-ratio recommendations. Use only bounded methods and qualitative handling observations.

[4] PMC6099745 (2018): Iranian farm ginger slices, open sun/solar tunnel/hot-air drying, HPLC gingerols/shogaols and five-hour Clevenger hydrodistillation oil yield. Drying endpoint 10±0.2% moisture. Temperature comparisons differ in drying duration to endpoint; not isolated temperature-only kinetics. No sensory panel, no beverage, no aroma component-resolved loss measurement. Bioactivity/antimicrobial results not used for product or preservative claims.
