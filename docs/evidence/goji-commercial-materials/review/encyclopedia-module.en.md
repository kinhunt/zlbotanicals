## Commercial goji materials: what three published listings do—and do not—establish

“Goji powder” can describe a food ingredient, an extracted fraction or a cosmetic ingredient brief. Start with intended use and composition—not a shared plant name or a supplier's headline percentage. The examples below are third-party market references, not ZL specifications or offers of the named products.

### Three selected commercial references, not a supplier ranking

| Market reference | What its own page actually declares | What the buyer still needs |
|---|---|---|
| Kalustyan’s Goji Berry / Wolfberry Juice Powder | Ingredients: “Goji berry juice powder, Maltodextrin.” [C4] | The carrier percentage and juice-solids contribution on a stated basis; whether the juice is clarified; reconstitution at the intended serving dilution. The ingredient list alone does not quantify fruit content. |
| ORGANICWAY Goji Extract Powder | Its three-format page distinguishes freeze-dried whole-fruit powder from extract powder and advertises the latter at ≥50% polysaccharides, with “UV” as the method shorthand. [C5] | A complete assay protocol, pretreatment, reference standard and reporting basis. The page combines “aqueous extraction” with “using only food-grade ethanol”; ask for the actual solvent sequence rather than resolving that wording by assumption. This is an attributed supplier claim, not a verified batch result. |
| Croda Fruitliquid™ Goji EC | INCI: “Water (and) Glycerin (and) Lycium Barbarum Fruit Extract”; listed applications include “Face / neck skin care”, “Body care”, “Hair conditioners - leave on”, “Hair conditioners - rinse off” and “Shampoos”. [C1] | The current technical data sheet and composition information needed to establish extract content, the preservation system, handling and use conditions. The public INCI listing does not give a dry-extract percentage or establish equivalence to a food powder. |

For a **fruit-drink or gummy brief**, identify juice powder versus milled whole fruit first. Obtain the complete ingredient composition before setting fruit-solids targets or comparing kilograms purchased. A declared drying carrier is not automatically a defect, but it is part of the formulation and must be counted.

For a **polysaccharide-oriented extract brief**, do not rank offers by “UV %” alone. In an original Lycium barbarum fractionation study, researchers removed monosaccharides and some oligosaccharides, separated fractions, and measured total saccharides using a phenol–sulfuric acid method with glucose as the standard. Fractions had no significant difference in total sugar content but differed in protein and polyphenol content. [S1] That experiment supports a practical procurement rule: request the material history and the method behind the number. It does not validate a commercial percentage or demonstrate that two supplier powders are interchangeable.

For a **liquid cosmetic brief**, provide the target INCI, leave-on or rinse-off use, destination market and any solvent or preservative exclusions. Compare the proposed material in the actual formulation for colour, odour, pH compatibility and preservation requirements. These are development checks, not Croda use recommendations. Do not replace a water/glycerin ingredient with an equal mass of dry food extract solely because both contain goji.

### What these three examples cannot establish

These are deliberately selected examples of material wording, not a representative market sample, supplier shortlist or price comparison. Kalustyan’s is a retail seller reference, not verified industrial manufacturing or bulk-supply evidence. Its ingredient declaration does not establish the drying process or carrier proportion. [C4]

ORGANICWAY also lists whole-fruit powder at ≥30% (standard), an alternative ≥25% grade, and a “high-polysaccharide version” without clearly mapping that version to a numerical specification. The page does **not** explicitly call the ≥25% grade the high-polysaccharide version; that mapping should not be invented. Its ≥50% extract and ≥30% powder minimum claims do not establish an actual 1.7-fold concentration ratio between supplied batches. “UV” alone does not establish a shared complete method. [C5]

The solvent wording is unresolved, not evidence of manufacturing misconduct: request the extraction and precipitation steps and the solvent used at each stage. The organic, facility and “clinical-grade” claims on the page have not been independently qualified here. A supplier webpage is not a certificate or a batch COA. [C5]

Fruitliquid™ Goji EC is a named Croda product, not a generic synonym for goji extract. Referencing it does not imply that ZL distributes it or supplies an equivalent. The archived INCI does not establish quantitative extract loading or a preservative-free claim. Cosmetic use, suitability and any proposed substitution require grade-specific technical and regulatory assessment. [C1]
