Two listings can carry the same words — water-soluble green tea extract — and still describe different materials. One arrives as a dry powder priced against laboratory assay values; the other is an extract already dissolved in water and glycerin, priced against the percentage used in a formula. Confirm which of the two is on offer before comparing specification and price, because the paperwork you need, and the mistakes you can make, differ for each.

## Why the two do not substitute

Both listings can carry the label "water soluble", and that phrase describes how the material behaves in water. The powder's figures come from laboratory testing of the dry material, and they travel with a method, a reporting basis and a carrier declaration. The liquid is already diluted in water and glycerin, so it is quoted by the percentage used in a formula, and one sample above declares itself non-standardized.<a href="#water-soluble-green-tea-ref-5">[5]</a> Carrying a powder's EGCG figure into a liquid enquiry asks for a value nobody measured, while adding an external-use liquid to a beverage formula brings in a preservative system plus an external-use label statement: both liquid samples reviewed here state for external use only.<a href="#water-soluble-green-tea-ref-1">[1]</a><a href="#water-soluble-green-tea-ref-4">[4]</a>

Documentation is what settles the choice. For a powder, the assay and its reporting basis decide whether two offers describe the same material; for a liquid, composition, extract concentration beside the recommended use level, and the external-use statement decide whether it can go into your product at all. The wording on a specification sheet also needs a method behind it: a total-polyphenol colour value alone gives neither the EGCG fraction nor evidence about caffeine, which is why the reading rules for these numbers sit with the ingredient's composition and testing research.<a href="/plant-extracts/ingredients/green-tea#components">Green tea composition and assay methods</a>

## What to request before you ask for a price

- Name the destination market, the application format, and which of the two forms you mean. A beverage brief and a skincare brief start from different documents.
- For a powder, request HPLC results for EGCG, total catechins and caffeine with the reporting basis stated, plus a carrier declaration and the batch COA for the offered lot.
- For a liquid, request the INCI and full composition, the extract concentration beside the recommended use level, the preservative system, stability data and the external-use statement.
- Keep the distinction between total polyphenols, individual catechins, EGCG and caffeine in view when setting marker priorities; constituents, applications and processing questions are covered in the <a href="/plant-extracts/ingredients/green-tea">green tea ingredient entry</a>, and the commercial offer, grades and packing terms sit on the <a href="/products/green-tea">green tea extract procurement page</a>.
