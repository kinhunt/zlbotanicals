## Choose a format for the application

Whole turmeric powder can bring spice character and insoluble solids; a concentrated extract has a different composition. A carrier-based dispersion adds another set of ingredients. Define the desired color, flavor, mouthfeel and label constraints before comparing marker assays.

## Read curcuminoid specifications carefully

Ask for curcumin, demethoxycurcumin and bisdemethoxycurcumin, the total calculation and the HPLC method. Establish whether results describe the whole powder or an extract before carrier addition. Request identity and risk-based testing for unauthorized colorants, lead and solvent residues.

## Test the cup and the dry blend

Use the intended dairy or plant base and actual preparation conditions. Compare wetting, clumping, sediment, staining, spice notes and color. In the powder, assess segregation, moisture pickup and flow. Retain samples in the intended packaging to evaluate marker retention and sensory changes.

## Separate dispersibility from absorption claims

A water-dispersible grade is not automatically truly soluble, and neither description demonstrates absorption in people. Do not transfer data from another branded ingredient to a new material. Ask a qualified reviewer to assess ingredient eligibility and any proposed claim independently.

## Questions to include in your RFQ

Send destination market, intended format, processing conditions, ingredient exclusions and sample needs. Ask for a current specification, batch-specific COA, full composition and risk-based contaminant testing. Specifications and availability are indicative until confirmed in writing; agree acceptance criteria and change-control terms before purchase.
