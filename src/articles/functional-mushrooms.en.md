<span id="market-size-and-outlook" class="anchor-alias" aria-hidden="true"></span>
The global **mushroom supplements market is forecast to reach <span class="amount">USD 8.83 billion</span> by 2031**, up from an estimated <span class="amount">USD 5.83 billion</span> in 2026, according to Mordor Intelligence, which projects an 8.66% CAGR for 2026–2031.<a href="#market-v3-ref-1" aria-label="Source 1">[1]</a>
The opportunity is becoming more specific than a broad wellness trend: U.S. retail data show rapid mainstream growth in a defined mushroom supplement category, cognitive positioning is gaining sales, and brands are taking mushrooms into coffee and ready-to-drink energy products.<a href="#market-v3-ref-2" aria-label="Source 2">[2]</a><a href="#market-v3-ref-5" aria-label="Source 5">[5]</a><a href="#market-v3-ref-11" aria-label="Source 11">[11]</a>

The commercial challenge is to turn that interest into repeat purchases and dependable ingredient orders. Capsules compete through a clear material and daily-use proposition; drinks must also earn a place in an existing routine. Upstream, consistency and application performance connect those propositions to scalable supply.

<h2 id="us-retail">Mainstream retail is catching up with an established specialty category</h2>

<!-- exhibit:2 -->

The mainstream category entered the report’s top 40 ingredients for the first time, while natural expanded ranked it second.<a href="#market-v3-ref-2" aria-label="Source 2">[2]</a>
The faster-growing channel is still the smaller one. This supports a two-part strategy: specialist retailers offer an established category audience, while mainstream expansion requires a proposition that shoppers can understand without extensive education. Sales growth alone does not reveal how much came from distribution, price or consumption.

**Cognitive positioning has become a substantial commercial entry point.** Within that same mainstream “mushrooms (other)” category, cognitive-health-positioned supplements generated <span class="amount">USD 7.65 million</span> in 2024, up 114.5% and accounting for nearly half of category sales; immune-health positioning accounted for 6.1%.<a href="#market-v3-ref-2" aria-label="Source 2">[2]</a>
SPINS classifies health focus from label statements: this is evidence of sales attached to a product proposition, not measured cognitive efficacy or a survey of purchase motives.<a href="#market-v3-ref-2" aria-label="Source 2">[2]</a>

Nor is the expansion uniform across mushrooms. In the natural expanded channel, the separately tracked cordyceps category declined 4.8% in 2024.<a href="#market-v3-ref-2" aria-label="Source 2">[2]</a>
Species-level planning therefore matters more than applying one mushroom growth rate to every purchasing forecast.

<h2 id="segments">Capsules provide scale; drinks change the competitive set</h2>

Mordor estimates capsules and tablets at 46.43% of global mushroom supplement revenue in 2025 and forecasts powder growth of 9.78% annually over 2026–2031.<a href="#market-v3-ref-1" aria-label="Source 1">[1]</a>
In its channel estimates, pharmacies and drug stores held 36.22% in 2025, while online retail has a projected 10.69% CAGR for 2026–2031.<a href="#market-v3-ref-1" aria-label="Source 1">[1]</a>
These estimates favor complementary product and distribution strategies, rather than a wholesale shift away from capsules.

**Capsules** offer a contained daily-use task: identify the mushroom material, explain the formulation and make routine use straightforward. **Coffee and drink mixes** compete for a morning cup and household beverage budget. **Ready-to-drink products** compete for an immediate energy occasion, where single-can trial, availability and replenishment matter.

The broader food-and-drink context helps explain the attraction—and difficulty—of the beverage route. NCA’s Fall 2025 survey summary reports that 82% of past-day coffee drinkers drank coffee at home.<a href="#market-v3-ref-4" aria-label="Source 4">[4]</a>
In IFIC’s general U.S. food-and-beverage survey, taste and price influenced purchase decisions more than healthfulness; price registered 71% and healthfulness 57% (ratings of four or five on a five-point impact scale).<a href="#market-v3-ref-3" aria-label="Source 3">[3]</a>
Neither survey measures mushroom-specific demand. Their relevance is the existing habit and purchase criteria that a mushroom drink must fit.

Four Sigmatic combines coffee and capsules in its Target assortment, while RYZE combines its six-mushroom blend with coffee and describes approximately 48 mg caffeine per cup.<a href="#market-v3-ref-5" aria-label="Source 5">[5]</a><a href="#market-v3-ref-6" aria-label="Source 6">[6]</a>
Odyssey takes a different route, offering energy-drink lines with 85 mg and 222 mg caffeine.<a href="#market-v3-ref-11" aria-label="Source 11">[11]</a>
Mushroom beverages are therefore not one low-caffeine substitution market. A brand must decide whether it replaces ordinary coffee, supplements an existing supplement routine, or challenges conventional energy drinks.

<h2 id="product-positioning">Seven brands compete through materials, formats and routines</h2>

*Official product and brand descriptions; positioning terms describe the offer, not an efficacy comparison.*

<!-- exhibit:4 -->

The main division is not simply “more mushrooms” versus “fewer mushrooms.” It is between **specified extracts, mycelium/full-spectrum materials and lifestyle-oriented combinations**. Real Mushrooms makes extraction and constituent content part of its pitch, while Host Defense foregrounds mycelium.<a href="#market-v3-ref-7" aria-label="Source 7">[7]</a><a href="#market-v3-ref-9" aria-label="Source 9">[9]</a>
Om and Four Sigmatic combine mushrooms with other functional ingredients.<a href="#market-v3-ref-10" aria-label="Source 10">[10]</a><a href="#market-v3-ref-5" aria-label="Source 5">[5]</a>

These are different purchasing propositions, not interchangeable milligram totals. An extraction ratio, actual extract mass and beta-glucan concentration describe different properties; none alone establishes clinical equivalence.<a href="#market-v3-ref-21" aria-label="Source 21">[21]</a>
For an ingredient supplier, the advantage lies in making its material both reproducible and easy for the brand to explain. For a brand, adding more species is less useful than making the intended use and composition coherent.

<h2 id="channels">Channel expansion creates different operating costs</h2>

Direct-to-consumer websites can explain materials, bundle products and schedule replenishment, as FreshCap’s subscription offer illustrates.<a href="#market-v3-ref-8" aria-label="Source 8">[8]</a>
Their commercial test is retention after the first order: acquisition spending and subscription discounts can consume the apparent retail margin.

Specialist retail allows a more detailed ingredient proposition; mainstream shelves require quicker recognition. Four Sigmatic’s presence in the Target assortment and a Whole Foods product listing provides a concrete example of those different retail contexts.<a href="#market-v3-ref-5" aria-label="Source 5">[5]</a><a href="#market-v3-ref-22" aria-label="Source 22">[22]</a>
The operating test is sales per store and repeat replenishment, rather than the number of doors announced.

Ready-to-drink distribution is more operationally demanding. BevNET’s 2024 reporting on Odyssey described regional direct-store-delivery partners, KeHE and UNFI availability, and McLane serving 7-Eleven accounts.<a href="#market-v3-ref-15" aria-label="Source 15">[15]</a>
The same report connected expansion with production scheduling, faster inventory turnover and work to lower ingredient costs.<a href="#market-v3-ref-15" aria-label="Source 15">[15]</a>
A wider footprint can increase working-capital requirements before it improves profitability.

That makes **delivered cost per serving** a better planning measure than retail price alone. Include the mushroom ingredient, other functional ingredients, coffee or beverage base, conversion, packaging, freight, channel deductions and acquisition or promotion costs. The right cost structure differs by channel; a high shelf price does not reveal an extract supplier’s margin.

<h2 id="extracts">Six value-chain layers turn cultivation into a repeatable product</h2>

| Layer | Where value is created | Commercial pressure |
|---|---|---|
| **Cultivation and harvesting** | Species, source material and predictable dried input; Nammex describes selected strains, substrates and Chinese cultivation partners.<a href="#market-v3-ref-13" aria-label="Source 13">[13]</a> | Biological variability and crop planning; cultivation origin is distinct from extraction and packing location. |
| **Extraction and standardization** | Convert variable biological material into a defined ingredient; Nammex describes fruiting-body extract powders and lot-level beta-D-glucan quantification.<a href="#market-v3-ref-12" aria-label="Source 12">[12]</a> | Yield, energy and specification consistency; a chemical specification alone does not establish beverage suitability. |
| **Quality release and cross-border supply** | Identity, contaminant control, traceability and release discipline; Nammex describes testing before export and in North America.<a href="#market-v3-ref-13" aria-label="Source 13">[13]</a><a href="#market-v3-ref-14" aria-label="Source 14">[14]</a> | Testing time, inventory held pending release and landed-cost exposure. |
| **Formulation and finished manufacture** | Convert the material into capsules, powders, gummies or shelf-stable drinks, as the brand examples demonstrate.<a href="#market-v3-ref-5" aria-label="Source 5">[5]</a><a href="#market-v3-ref-10" aria-label="Source 10">[10]</a><a href="#market-v3-ref-11" aria-label="Source 11">[11]</a> | Sensory trade-offs, manufacturing yield and stability; owning a brand does not establish ownership of a factory. |
| **Brand and demand generation** | Explain a distinctive use, earn trial and encourage repeat purchase. | Acquisition cost, proposition overlap and excessive SKU complexity. |
| **Distribution and retail** | Shelf access, availability and replenishment, illustrated by Odyssey’s distributor network.<a href="#market-v3-ref-15" aria-label="Source 15">[15]</a> | Channel fees, slow-moving inventory, stockouts and returns. |

Nammex’s turkey-tail cultivation project shows how demand can reach the farm. A 2024 trade report described dried output with Chinese partner farms rising from 12,000 kg in 2022 to 30,000 kg in 2023—an increase of 150%.<a href="#market-v3-ref-14" aria-label="Source 14">[14]</a>
This is a company project’s reported production, not an industry growth rate. Its significance is the link between a species-specific commercial opportunity and cultivation planning.

To translate finished demand into ingredient requirements, use **sold servings × actual ingredient grams per serving ÷ 1,000 = ingredient kilograms**. Procurement planning then adds manufacturing losses and inventory changes. A blend also requires species proportions; its total mass cannot be assigned to each mushroom. Extract demand can only be converted into dried-mushroom demand with an applicable process yield, not a retail “raw equivalent” claim.

<h2 id="north-america">Regional expansion can require a different material, not just a translated label</h2>

Mordor estimates North America at 34.57% of global mushroom supplement revenue in 2025 and projects Asia-Pacific growth of 9.96% annually over 2026–2031.<a href="#market-v3-ref-1" aria-label="Source 1">[1]</a>
Its North American scope includes the United States, Canada and Mexico.<a href="#market-v3-ref-1" aria-label="Source 1">[1]</a>
The U.S. channel evidence above provides a concrete basis for testing products; it does not establish a comparable country ranking for the rest of the world.

**United States:** the observed opportunity spans specialist supplements and mainstream retail. Product development still needs the applicable food or supplement route and supportable claims; FDA does not approve dietary supplements for safety and effectiveness before marketing under DSHEA.<a href="#market-v3-ref-20" aria-label="Source 20">[20]</a>

**Canada:** a reconstituted beverage powder may be classified as food, with composition, presentation, format and history of use informing the decision.<a href="#market-v3-ref-16" aria-label="Source 16">[16]</a>
Products meeting the natural health product definition require a product licence before legal sale.<a href="#market-v3-ref-17" aria-label="Source 17">[17]</a>
A coffee project and a dosed NHP project should therefore be planned separately, with a local party responsible for the applicable route.

**European Union:** a 2019 consultation classified dehydrated lion’s mane mycelium powder as novel food.<a href="#market-v3-ref-18" aria-label="Source 18">[18]</a>
A 2025 consultation found the submitted lion’s mane fruiting-body extract powder—hot-water and ultrasound-assisted extraction followed by freeze-drying, for use in foods and food supplements—not novel. The evidence submitted showed no relevant significant compositional or structural change compared with traditional water extraction.<a href="#market-v3-ref-19" aria-label="Source 19">[19]</a>
That finding concerns the described material and process, not all lion’s mane extracts or permission for health claims. Species, part and process must be matched before committing to a cross-border formulation.

<h2 id="implications">The strongest opportunities pair a specific task with an economic test</h2>

- **Cognitive-positioned supplements:** start with a coherent primary use and a documented material proposition, rather than a long species list. The U.S. sales signal makes this a credible test direction.<a href="#market-v3-ref-2" aria-label="Source 2">[2]</a>
Brands should evaluate repeat purchase and net contribution; suppliers should provide consistent identity, constituent specifications and batch documentation. The main risk is an indistinct proposition that relies on ever-stronger claims to win attention.
- **Home coffee and drink mixes:** develop for a specific preparation method and beverage base. Existing coffee habits and the Four Sigmatic/RYZE examples support the route.<a href="#market-v3-ref-4" aria-label="Source 4">[4]</a><a href="#market-v3-ref-5" aria-label="Source 5">[5]</a><a href="#market-v3-ref-6" aria-label="Source 6">[6]</a>
Prioritize blind taste comparison, preparation success and cost per cup before proliferating flavors. Ingredient suppliers can differentiate with performance in the actual base, not a generic “soluble” description.
- **Gummies and ready-to-drink products:** convenience broadens the offer, but added ingredients and processing compete for formulation space and cost.<a href="#market-v3-ref-10" aria-label="Source 10">[10]</a><a href="#market-v3-ref-11" aria-label="Source 11">[11]</a>
Establish loading, sensory acceptance and shelf-life performance before scaling; for RTD, connect production runs to realistic replenishment and inventory forecasts. Odyssey’s experience makes supply-chain efficiency part of the growth story, not a later correction.<a href="#market-v3-ref-15" aria-label="Source 15">[15]</a>

The likely durable advantage is not the broadest mushroom portfolio. It is a product consumers can understand and keep using, supported by a material and supply chain that can reproduce the promise at an acceptable delivered cost.
