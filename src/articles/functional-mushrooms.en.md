Mordor Intelligence estimates the global **mushroom supplements market at USD 5.83 billion in 2026**, rising to a forecast USD 8.83 billion in 2031. Capsules and tablets anchor its 2025 revenue mix, while powders have a faster growth forecast.<a href="#market-ref-1" aria-label="Source 1">[1]</a>

This overview covers published supplement estimates, a separate reishi-extract estimate and observed product positioning—not a unified functional-mushroom market total.

<span id="growth-segments-capsules-powders-and-distribution" class="anchor-alias"></span>
<h2 id="segments">Formats and channels: established share, forecast growth</h2>

<h3>2025 revenue structure</h3>

<!-- exhibit:2 -->

**Capsules and tablets remain the largest format in Mordor’s 2025 estimate**, accounting for 46.43% of supplement revenue.<a href="#market-ref-1" aria-label="Source 1">[1]</a> The publisher connects their position with familiar presentation, dosing convenience and pharmacy purchasing habits.<a href="#market-ref-1" aria-label="Source 1">[1]</a> For brands, this makes capsules a relevant format for both a clearly identified single mushroom and a multi-mushroom formulation.

<h3>2026–2031 growth outlook</h3>

<!-- exhibit:3 -->

**Powders have the faster forecast growth.** Mordor projects a 9.78% CAGR through 2031 and links this expansion to beverages, smoothies, coffee and other daily-use occasions.<a href="#market-ref-1" aria-label="Source 1">[1]</a> This points to an opportunity to develop products around preparation and consumption habits, alongside conventional capsules. It also makes the distinction between packaged extract powder and a multi-ingredient drink mix commercially useful: they present different products to the shopper.

Distribution adds another dimension. Mordor estimates that pharmacies and drug stores accounted for 36.22% of global supplement revenue in 2025, while online retail is forecast to grow at 10.69% CAGR through 2031.<a href="#market-ref-1" aria-label="Source 1">[1]</a> These findings suggest complementary priorities: a concise, recognizable product proposition for retail shelves, and fuller explanations of mushroom material and formulation online. The channel figures are global, not a North American or Amazon-specific split.

<h3 id="extracts">Medicinal mushroom extract market: materials and end uses</h3>

For readers examining the medicinal mushroom extract market, material identity and end use are as important as the report title. The reishi estimate covers several applications, while the supplement estimate above concerns finished products; the two are not a bulk-ingredient market total.<a href="#market-ref-1">[1]</a><a href="#market-ref-2">[2]</a>



The observed reishi products span fruiting-body extract capsules, liquid drops and an extract powder.<a href="#market-ref-3" aria-label="Source 3">[3]</a><a href="#market-ref-6" aria-label="Source 6">[6]</a><a href="#market-ref-7" aria-label="Source 7">[7]</a> This gives brands alternatives to competing only through a longer ingredient list: a focused mushroom identity can be expressed through different formats and material descriptions. Micro Ingredients’ listing also shows why “powder” should not automatically be read as unextracted whole-mushroom powder—it explicitly describes an extract.<a href="#market-ref-7" aria-label="Source 7">[7]</a>

For ingredient suppliers, useful differentiation lies in documented species and source material, measured constituent content and suitability for the intended format. An extraction ratio is not itself a beta-glucan concentration or a measure of efficacy; material fractions and composition require their own specification.<a href="#market-ref-10" aria-label="Source 10">[10]</a> Capsule ingredients and drink ingredients also face different practical requirements, from filling behavior to dispersion and taste.<a href="#market-ref-10" aria-label="Source 10">[10]</a> A format-specific material offering gives the brand a more concrete proposition than a generic extract grade.

<span id="north-america-and-uscanada-regulatory-differences" class="anchor-alias"></span>
<h2 id="north-america">North America: supplement demand and national market entry</h2>

Mordor estimates that **North America accounted for 34.57% of global mushroom supplement revenue in 2025**, making it the largest region in that study.<a href="#market-ref-1" aria-label="Source 1">[1]</a> Its geographic coverage includes the United States, Canada, Mexico and the rest of North America.<a href="#market-ref-1" aria-label="Source 1">[1]</a> By contrast, its 2025 reishi-extract report identifies Asia-Pacific as the largest region for that separate market.<a href="#market-ref-2" aria-label="Source 2">[2]</a>

For brands considering North America, the regional scale makes finished supplements a relevant focus. The capsule, blend, powder and drop examples above provide concrete product propositions to consider, while the global channel outlook highlights both pharmacy retail and online product education. The evidence here does not establish a U.S.–Canada sales split or confirm U.S. delivery for the Amazon listings.

For the North America medicinal mushroom extract market—and the U.S. market specifically—the evidence reviewed here does not establish a separate extract-revenue estimate. The 34.57% regional share refers to mushroom supplements across North America, not U.S. extract sales.<a href="#market-ref-1">[1]</a>

<h3 id="us-canada">United States and Canada: regulatory differences</h3>

The regulatory distinction is more specific. In the **United States**, FDA states that it is not authorized under DSHEA to approve dietary supplements for safety and effectiveness before marketing.<a href="#market-ref-8" aria-label="Source 8">[8]</a> Listing a supplement for sale is therefore not evidence of FDA approval. In **Canada**, products classified as natural health products fall under the Natural Health Products Regulations, with product licensing, site licensing and GMP requirements relevant to market entry.<a href="#market-ref-9" aria-label="Source 9">[9]</a> The applicable obligations depend on the product and business activity; a regional commercial strategy still needs country-specific classification and label work.

<h2 id="implications">Implications for product and ingredient strategy</h2>

The strongest practical takeaway is to pair a clear material proposition with a relevant product format: capsules remain substantial in the publisher’s estimate, powders have a faster growth forecast, and the observed listings demonstrate several distinct formulation choices.
