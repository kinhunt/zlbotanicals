### Changing Stevia Production Routes: What to Compare When Both Materials Say “95%”

When replacing leaf-extracted steviol glycosides with enzymatically produced Reb M or a fermentation-derived ingredient, price and “≥95% total glycosides” are not enough. Different EU entries anchor their assays to eleven glycosides together, Reb M alone, Reb D plus Reb A, or a total that includes glucosylated derivatives. The same percentage does not necessarily measure the same substances.<a href="#i07-route-ref-1">[1]</a>

A useful substitution review separates three questions: **which identity definition fits the replacement, which specification or analytical evidence needs updating, and whether the material meets agreed performance criteria in the actual formulation.** The comparisons below help plan supplier requests and sample evaluation; they do not assume that every route change invalidates every document or requires every test to be repeated.

> **Version boundary:** EU specification examples refer to the **18 August 2026 consolidation** of Regulation (EU) No 231/2012. On 27 September 2026, the EUR-Lex base-act page identified it as the current consolidated version. Consolidations are documentation tools; the authentic legal texts remain the acts and amendments published in the Official Journal.<a href="#i07-route-ref-1">[1]</a><a href="#i07-route-ref-2">[2]</a><a href="#i07-route-ref-3">[3]</a> Chinese examples are limited to the 2024 announcement attachment and the 2025 issue 11 gazette; US examples concern named GRAS notice records; Australian/New Zealand examples carry their document dates. This article does not establish current maximum use levels or market access for a particular finished food.

#### 1. Specify what the “95%” measures

This table compares identity and assay definitions in the EU **18 August 2026 version**. It does not establish that a particular supplier’s material fits an entry.<a href="#i07-route-ref-1">[1]</a>

<div class="i07-table-scroll" role="region" tabindex="0" aria-label="Assay definitions">

| Route and entry | Starting material and process definition | How to read the assay |
|---|---|---|
| Leaf extraction, E 960a | Water extraction of stevia leaves, preliminary purification and recrystallisation | At least 95% of eleven listed glycosides on the dried basis, in any combination and ratio—not at least 95% Reb M.<a href="#i07-route-ref-1">[1]</a> |
| Fermentation, E 960b(i) | A simple sugar source fermented by *Yarrowia lipolytica* VRM (CBS 147477), followed by purification | At least 95% Reb M, D, A and B together on the dried basis—not at least 95% Reb M alone.<a href="#i07-route-ref-1">[1]</a><a href="#i07-route-ref-4">[4]</a> |
| Enzymatic conversion, E 960c(i) and (ii) | Enzymatic treatment of leaf-derived glycosides; the entries describe different substrates and enzyme-production organisms | At least 95% Reb M on the dried basis. The process definition must also match; assay alone cannot select the entry.<a href="#i07-route-ref-1">[1]</a> |
| Enzymatic conversion, E 960c(iii) and (iv) | (iii) converts highly purified Reb A leaf extracts; (iv) converts highly purified stevioside leaf extracts | (iii): at least 95% Reb D plus Reb A on the dried basis; (iv): at least 95% Reb AM on the dried basis.<a href="#i07-route-ref-1">[1]</a> |
| Glucosylation, E 960d | Leaf-derived glycosides and starch treated with the specified enzyme, producing glucosylated derivatives and residual parent glycosides | At least 95% total glycosides on a **dried, dextrin-free basis**, including derivatives with 1–20 added glucose units—not the Reb M content of the powder as supplied.<a href="#i07-route-ref-1">[1]</a> |

</div>

For quotation comparisons, separate total glycosides, principal individual glycosides, moisture or loss on drying, carriers and the reporting basis. A dry-basis assay is not automatically the constituent mass in each kilogram of supplied powder. Obtain composition data on a comparable basis before comparing ingredient cost.

**An enzyme made by fermentation does not make the final glycoside a sugar-fermentation product.** The E 960c definitions above start from leaf-derived glycosides, whereas E 960b(i) starts with fermentation of a simple sugar source. Follow the complete substrate-to-product process, rather than the phrase “fermentation technology”.<a href="#i07-route-ref-1">[1]</a>

#### 2. Let specific specification differences determine the evidence request

All numerical examples in this section come from the same EU **18 August 2026 consolidation**. They explain why an old COA template needs review before use for a replacement; they are not a universal test package for every market.<a href="#i07-route-ref-1">[1]</a><a href="#i07-route-ref-3">[3]</a>

<div class="i07-table-scroll" role="region" tabindex="0" aria-label="Specification differences">

| Check | Specific difference in that version | Implication for substitution |
|---|---|---|
| Residual protein | E 960b(i): ≤20 mg/kg; E 960c(i)–(iv): ≤5 mg/kg | Request the applicable entry, method and detection capability. A generic “low protein” statement does not replace the relevant specification.<a href="#i07-route-ref-1">[1]</a> |
| Particle size | E 960b(i): no more than 0.5% of particles below 300 nm, by STEM; E 960c(i)–(iv): not less than 74 μm, using a #200 sieve | A nanoparticle proportion and a sieve-based lower size limit are different measurements. Reports are not interchangeable just because both say “particle size”.<a href="#i07-route-ref-1">[1]</a> |
| Metals | E 960a: arsenic and lead each ≤1 mg/kg; E 960b(i): arsenic, lead and cadmium each ≤0.01 mg/kg, mercury ≤0.05 mg/kg; E 960c(i)–(iv): arsenic, lead, cadmium and mercury respectively ≤0.015, 0.2, 0.015 and 0.07 mg/kg | Compare applicable limits first, then assess whether the method’s quantification capability and the report are adequate.<a href="#i07-route-ref-1">[1]</a> |
| Residual solvents | E 960a lists methanol ≤200 and ethanol ≤5000 mg/kg; E 960c(i)–(iv) list ethanol ≤5000 mg/kg; E 960d lists methanol ≤200 and ethanol ≤3000 mg/kg | Compare the actual solvent-use history with the applicable specification. “Not listed in this entry” does not mean “never needs assessment”.<a href="#i07-route-ref-1">[1]</a> |
| Microbiology | E 960b(i) and E 960d list microbiological criteria; the E 960a and E 960c(i)–(iv) entries do not | Distinguish entry-specific criteria from other applicable requirements or customer specifications. An omitted criterion is not a statutory exemption.<a href="#i07-route-ref-1">[1]</a> |
| Production-organism controls | The definitions of E 960b(i) and E 960c(i)–(iv) require that viable cells and DNA of the specified production organisms not be detected in the additive | Evidence must correspond to the actual organism and process. An ordinary total plate count does not replace production-organism viability or DNA assessment.<a href="#i07-route-ref-1">[1]</a> |

</div>

E 960b(i) also specifies kaurenoic acid at ≤0.3 mg/kg, measured by liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry.<a href="#i07-route-ref-1">[1]</a><a href="#i07-route-ref-4">[4]</a> Route-specific items like this can be missed by a generic “steviol glycosides certificate”.

##### Do not turn an applicant’s specification in a scientific opinion into a release standard

The EFSA opinion adopted on 18 November 2025 and published in 2026 considers the applicant’s proposed SBP1 and SBP2 entries. Table 1 proposes a residual-protein maximum of **75 mg/kg for SBP1**; Table 2 proposes **30 mg/kg for SBP2**. The 75 mg/kg value cannot be applied to both products. The 20 μm particle-size criterion is also an applicant proposal. The Panel considered introducing the proposed methanol and ethanol limits unnecessary.<a href="#i07-route-ref-5">[5]</a>

The retrieved **18 August 2026 consolidation** still lists the 5 mg/kg residual-protein and 74 μm particle-size criteria for E 960c(i)–(iv), without SBP1/SBP2 entries.<a href="#i07-route-ref-1">[1]</a><a href="#i07-route-ref-3">[3]</a> The scientific opinion therefore cannot replace that version’s specifications. If a supplier relies on a later change, request the formal act, entry and applicable date.

#### 3. Market-specific records are not interchangeable by number

##### China: match the specification, then check the food category separately

The 2024 announcement attachment for “甜菊糖苷（酶转化法）” specifies Reb M at ≥95.0% on the dried basis, with lead and arsenic each ≤1.0 mg/kg in Table 2. **These are enzymatic-route criteria, not evidence of Chinese leaf-extract limits.** The attachment also supplies a use table and production-organism information.<a href="#i07-route-ref-8">[8]</a>

The supplementary-specification section of the 2025 issue 11 gazette gives an enzymatic-route organism table using *E. coli* K-12 and refers the other quality specifications for that item to Announcement 2024 No. 2. It separately describes fermentation-derived material, including glucose as substrate, an inactivation step and total steviol glycosides at ≥95.0% on the dried basis.<a href="#i07-route-ref-9">[9]</a> Request the announcement, organism and donor information corresponding to the supplier’s actual process; do not treat organism tables from different announcements as interchangeable attachments for one process.

Chinese leaf-extract limits are not reproduced here, and these historical documents are not presented as an exhaustive current market-access list. Obtain the applicable official standards and announcements, then have the regulatory reviewer check food categories, use levels, steviol-equivalent calculations and labelling for the actual product.

##### United States: a record concerns a substance and use, not an automatic “new GRN” for every substitution

FDA’s GRN 1010 inventory record concerns Manus Bio’s Reb M obtained by enzymatic treatment of leaf-derived glycosides, with a closure date of 26 January 2022. Its intended use is as a general-purpose sweetener, excluding infant formula and meat and poultry products, at levels determined by good manufacturing practices. GRN 745 concerns PureCircle’s steviol glycosides consisting primarily of Reb M, closed on 20 April 2018. Both records state “FDA has no questions”.<a href="#i07-route-ref-6">[6]</a><a href="#i07-route-ref-7">[7]</a>

These two records do not establish that every supplier or route change requires a new GRN. Request an explanation of **how the actual material, composition, process and uses correspond to the GRAS basis cited by the supplier**, together with supporting documents. This article does not determine that a replacement is covered, or describe an inventory status as FDA approval of all stevia products.

##### Australia and New Zealand: molecular identity and purity do not alone settle process fit

FSANZ’s **12 May 2021 A1207 approval report** provides a concrete example: Amyris’s fermentation-derived Reb M met the described purity parameters but not the particular production method in the existing specification, prompting an amendment to that specification. The case concerned production method, without extending existing permitted food categories or maximum use levels.<a href="#i07-route-ref-11">[11]</a>

FSANZ’s consumer explanation, updated **26 November 2025**, distinguishes leaf extraction, enzymatic conversion of leaf extracts and fermentation from sugar, while giving labelling examples such as “sweetener (960)”.<a href="#i07-route-ref-10">[10]</a> Consumer ingredient wording therefore need not identify the manufacturing route; procurement still needs the process dossier. Neither document replaces a current Code check for a particular purchased material, and EU E-number suffixes should not be imported as Australian/New Zealand labelling requirements.

#### 4. An actionable review: separate documents, samples and decisions

The following is procurement and quality-management advice, not a legally prescribed universal retest list.

<div class="i07-table-scroll" role="region" tabindex="0" aria-label="Substitution decisions">

| Question to resolve | Supplier evidence to request | Sample work and decision |
|---|---|---|
| Do the old and new materials fit the same specification object? | Old/new process flows, substrates, principal glycosides, organism or enzyme information, applicable entry and version | Quality and regulatory reviewers check identity and method applicability before selecting additional identity or profile work. |
| Are the replacement’s reports sufficient for acceptance? | Itemised specifications, representative-lot COAs, methods and quantification limits, explanations of deviations | Select verification work according to entry differences and supplier risk—not automatic reuse of the old COA or indiscriminate repetition of every test. |
| Are quotations comparable? | Composition, carrier and moisture information on the same as-supplied basis, plus actual quotations | Compare purchase cost separately from the dose needed to meet the formulation target. Do not calculate savings directly from regulatory sweetness descriptions. |
| Does the material work in the product? | Traceable sample lot numbers, lot composition and suggested application conditions | R&D compares dissolution, sedimentation, sweetness and aftertaste in the same base, dosing procedure and process; schedule storage observation according to project risk. |
| Do existing certificates or claims still apply? | Certificate scope and claim support corresponding to the new substrates, organisms, enzymes and purification steps | Ask the relevant body whether updated documentation or procedures are needed. Do not assume every route change requires a fresh application, or that DNA nondetection establishes a non-GMO claim. |
| When may supply switch? | First-supply specification, agreed change-notification terms, lot traceability and old-stock arrangements | Close regulatory/documentary review, quality acceptance and application evaluation separately. A successful taste screen does not settle the other decisions. |

</div>

The most useful supplier response is not “ours is also Reb M”. It identifies **what this lot is, which documents govern acceptance, which changes are supported by evidence and which applications still need sample validation**. With that information, substitution rates and commercial terms can be compared on a common basis.
